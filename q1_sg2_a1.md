Scenario
The PSHS school canteen is small and often gets crowded during lunch break. Students line up to buy food, but the process is slow because:
Some students take too long to decide what to order.
The cashier has to manually calculate totals and give change.
There is no system to track which food items are running out.
Your group’s task is to decompose this problem into smaller, manageable parts that could be solved with computational thinking (CT) Skills.

Step 1: Identify the Big Problem
Main Problem: The main issue lies on the overall inefficiency, crowdedness, and disorganization in the canteen operations during lunch breaks. The evident proof of a lacking system and competent arrangements results in a sluggish and ultimately inconvenient service experience. Since everything in the canteen is a manual process, the canteen struggles to effectively handle a large crowd of students, especially with the limited lunch period. Since everything is conducted manually, managing food inventory and taking every transaction would naturally take longer—causing disorganization and long queues.


Step 2: Identify three to four Sub-Problems
Please list possible sub-problems:
1. A prominent subproblem is in the inability of students to order at a standard, fast pace. This results in traffic jams, not only due to the time burnt on choosing the meal or viand, but in the preparation of it as well.
2. Another issue that emerged is in the inconvenience brought to the cashier due to the need of manual arithmetic calculation for meal totals. This, combined with the formerly stated issue, results in a very inefficient processing of queues.
3. The next obstacle that became apparent is in the absence of a real-time inventory system that would track what was and wasn’t available.
4. Beyond the listed observations of surfaced difficulties, an underlying factor is secretly wasting minutes. This is in the disorganization or chaos formed when the queues get too long and crowded. This results in the further need of being cramped between multiple people, discouraging purchase of meals just to avoid the inconvenience.

Step 3: Define Computational Thinking Approaches
For each sub-problem, apply CT skills:
Sub-Problem
CT Skill
Example Solution
Slow order times
Abstraction
Create a simple, clarified menu that filters unnecessary complexity by emphasizing what is essential. Remove low-sales viands and possibly include meal combos to decrease order times. This aligns with the CT skill abstraction because it only shows the key and important parts while removing low-level details.
Lack of automated arithmetic calculations
Algorithm Design
Make a basic arithmetic program that would simply ask for inputs and then calculate change from the difference of amount paid and total (Change = Amount Paid - Amount Due). This should be accessible via any mobile device that the cashier has—processing amounts in sequential steps reminiscent of algorithm design.
Absence of a real-time inventory system
Pattern Recognition
Record and log the depletion rates of each viand provided. Analyze these sale trends after, in order to conclude which items are in the highest demand, and therefore must have the most volume. This utilizes the CT skill of pattern recognition as accumulated data is associated with what is anticipated.
Direct inconvenience in physically being crammed in a line
Decomposition
Divide the purchasing process into multiple, discrete checkpoints. This may include an initial ordering station, a separate pickup counter, and an immediate path for pre-orders. This breaks down the queue into smaller, more manageable parts—resembling the CT skill of decomposition.
